# Binance Square Top-Creator Deep Research Notes

**Research date:** 2026-09-08  
**Purpose:** Convert current Binance Square guidance into an engineering/editorial operating system for this repository.

## Executive conclusion

The current BTC post is **not yet top-creator quality**. It has useful ingredients—real news attribution, a specific BTC price, and an attempt to connect stablecoin adoption to market liquidity—but it reads like compressed AI market copy. The weakest pieces are the duplicated `Source:` construction, the abstract jump from stablecoin wallets to BTC breakout potential, lack of visible supporting data for that causal link, and the generic closing question `Bullish, bearish, or wait / Which asset...`.

A top creator cannot win by being a faster headline summarizer. The system must consistently produce an **original thesis supported by evidence**, explain an under-obvious mechanism, show what would confirm or invalidate the thesis, and use visuals to make the reasoning easier to verify.

This conclusion is grounded in current Binance Square / CreatorPad material and is an editorial target, not a guarantee of leaderboard rank or earnings.

## 1. What Binance currently signals that it values

### Creativity / originality

Binance's CreatorPad materials say originality is a key component. They distinguish original analysis and fresh perspectives from copied or substantially similar content. They also warn against AI-generated templates, repost-style writing and simply repeating a news report.

**Engineering implication:** every opportunity must be scored for information advantage and semantic originality before publication. A story with many existing similar Square posts should usually require a stronger differentiated angle.

### Professionalism / depth

Binance describes professionalism in terms of depth, structure, logical consistency, evidence and useful multimedia. It specifically favors deep sector/project analysis, educational explanations and event interpretation that explains implications and risk rather than simply naming a trend.

**Engineering implication:** a factual headline is not enough. The writing system must build a thesis from evidence and include a mechanism, implication and invalidation condition.

### Relevance

Binance's published scoring guidance emphasizes relevance to current crypto topics and related fields such as macroeconomics, policy and traditional finance intersections.

**Engineering implication:** detect why a story matters now, but do not manufacture urgency.

### Visual information

Binance says quality assessment considers the main content, visuals and trading widgets together. Official guidance repeatedly encourages useful charts/graphics and warns against decorative or fake visuals.

**Engineering implication:** the visual is part of the argument. Every post should declare what the visual proves or clarifies.

### Genuine engagement

Current Binance guidance says normal interactions from real users matter, while engagement farming is detected and can be deprioritized. Repetitive or artificial engagement is therefore a strategic dead end.

**Engineering implication:** the question at the end must open a real analytical conversation—not request a reaction for its own sake. Comment replies should add information.

### AI-generated content risk

Current Binance CreatorPad material explicitly says purely AI-generated content can be de-boosted and that original, thoughtful analysis is expected.

**Engineering implication:** the model must not impersonate personal experience or fabricate human stories. Instead, the system should use AI as an editorial/research engine that synthesizes supplied evidence into genuinely differentiated analysis. The objective is not to hide that automation exists; it is to avoid producing low-effort AI template output.

## 2. Current monetization implications

Binance's current Write to Earn announcement says eligible KYC-verified creators can earn commissions from qualifying reader trading activity after interacting with content, with published materials describing a base 20% commission and up to 50% for top-ranked creators, subject to eligibility and terms.

**Engineering implication:** monetization should be a downstream consequence of trust and useful reader action. Never distort analysis to push a trade, chase clicks, or create artificial activity.

## 3. Diagnosis of our posted BTC example

### What worked

The post had:
- a live BTC price observation
- a real source attribution
- a relevant stablecoin/financial-infrastructure theme
- an attempt to connect a macro/market-structure development to BTC

### What failed

**Hook:** `Fiat capital settling natively on-chain removes` is incomplete and abstract. It does not tell the reader what changed or why they should care.

**Source formatting:** `Source:` appears multiple times and makes the post look pipeline-generated rather than editorially composed.

**Thesis gap:** the jump from `stablecoin wallets challenge bank accounts` to `BTC breakout` is asserted more than demonstrated. The system needs evidence connecting the two.

**Market sentence:** `$BTC is holding near $78,403...` is useful only when the range/compression is quantified or visualized. A raw price alone is weak insight.

**Engagement:** `Bullish, bearish, or wait` is generic. `Which asset is giving the clearest confirmation?` is also broad and disconnected from a precise decision framework.

**Reader value:** the reader is told that on-chain liquidity could matter, but not what observable evidence would prove that the effect is reaching BTC.

**Competitive differentiation:** many creators could publish substantially the same post after reading the same headline.

## 4. The new content standard

Every post should answer all six questions:

1. What changed?
2. Why does it matter now?
3. What is the non-obvious implication?
4. What evidence supports that implication?
5. What would make the thesis wrong?
6. What should the reader watch next?

A post missing #3 or #4 is usually a recap, not insight.

A post missing #5 is usually too confident.

A post missing #6 creates no reusable decision framework.

## 5. The thesis engine

Before writing, produce one internal thesis sentence:

> The important development is X; evidence Y changes how we should interpret Z because mechanism M connects them.

Then produce the invalidation sentence:

> This interpretation fails if evidence Q occurs or if expected relationship R does not appear.

The system should reject any thesis that cannot be expressed clearly.

## 6. The information-advantage test

Before publication compare the planned post against:
- the source headline
- the source summary
- recent Square posts on the topic
- our own recent publications

The post should add at least one of:
- a new relationship
- a derived observation
- a mechanism explanation
- a contradiction between narrative and data
- a useful comparison
- a risk that is being overlooked
- a follow-up observation from a prior thesis
- a simple framework readers can reuse

If it adds none of these, **WAIT**.

## 7. The hook system

A strong hook should contain tension or a specific observation.

Preferred families:
- `The headline says X. The live data says Y.`
- `The surprising part of this move is not X—it is Y.`
- `One metric is changing before price. That matters because...`
- `Everyone is watching X. The cleaner signal may be Y.`
- `This looks like a normal BTC range—until you isolate Y.`

Avoid:
- `🚨 HUGE NEWS`
- `BTC is ready to...`
- ticker + percentage templates
- vague macro statements
- generic motivational language
- unfinished fragments

The hook must be complete, concrete and understandable without reading the rest.

## 8. The evidence ladder

Use evidence in this order whenever available:

**Level 1 — Primary:** official announcements, original datasets, exchange/issuer documentation, official project disclosures, direct market data.

**Level 2 — High-quality reporting:** specialist media with clear sourcing.

**Level 3 — Derived observation:** a calculation or relationship derived from verified raw inputs.

**Level 4 — Interpretation:** the creator's reasoned explanation of what the evidence could mean.

**Level 5 — Hypothesis:** clearly labeled and paired with what would falsify it.

Never present Levels 4–5 as Level 1 facts.

## 9. The visual rule

The chart should answer the same question as the prose.

Examples:
- If thesis = liquidity is expanding but price has not repriced, chart liquidity proxy beside price.
- If thesis = range compression matters, chart range width and volume around the event.
- If thesis = narrative and data diverge, show both in one visual.
- If thesis = event impact is delayed, mark the event timestamp and subsequent structure.

Do not generate a generic coin logo or AI illustration when a truthful data visual is available.

## 10. Engagement architecture

The closing question should identify a real analytical choice.

Bad:
- What do you think?
- Bullish or bearish?
- Which coin are you watching?
- Agree or disagree?

Better:
- `What evidence would convince you that the liquidity shift is actually reaching BTC rather than staying inside stablecoin activity?`
- `Would you treat sustained volume expansion or a break of the current range as the stronger confirmation here, and why?`

The question should be impossible to answer well without understanding the post.

## 11. Creator voice

The account should develop a recognizable editorial personality:
- decisive when evidence is decisive
- skeptical when the causal chain is weak
- excited by genuinely unusual data
- comfortable saying `I don't think the evidence is there yet`
- willing to disagree with the dominant narrative without manufacturing contrarianism

Do not fabricate personal trading experience, private conversations, profits, losses or emotions.

## 12. Story portfolio

The creator should not become a BTC-news bot. Maintain a portfolio:

### Market intelligence
Unusual structure, volume, volatility, liquidity and market relationships.

### News intelligence
What important news changes, what it does not change, and what evidence should follow.

### Mechanism explainers
Explain how a protocol, market structure or financial mechanism actually works using current examples.

### Risk intelligence
Identify hidden assumptions, invalidation conditions and misleading narratives.

### Original research
Derived observations that are not simply copied from a source.

### Follow-up intelligence
Return to a previous post and update the thesis when new evidence arrives.

## 13. Competition strategy

Do not compete only on speed.

When 20 creators share the same headline, the system should explicitly ask:

> What can we explain that the headline itself cannot?

Then prioritize that answer.

The strongest long-term moat is **memory**: remembering what was previously claimed, what evidence appeared later, what readers misunderstood, and which formats produced genuine value.

## 14. Learning system

Every post is an experiment.

Record:
- opportunity type
- thesis
- hook family
- evidence density
- visual type
- content length
- topic
- market regime
- audience signals
- views
- qualified interactions
- comments and recurring questions
- follows where measurable
- cashtag/widget interactions where measurable
- earnings where accessible
- CreatorPad points/rank where accessible

Do not infer causality from one post. Compare groups of posts and, where possible, similar opportunities under different editorial choices.

## 15. Autonomous decision policy

**PUBLISH** when opportunity quality, evidence, originality, audience fit and expected reader value are high.

**RESEARCH** when the idea may be strong but evidence is incomplete.

**REWORK** when the evidence is good but thesis, hook, narrative or visual is weak.

**WAIT** when the story is ordinary, duplicated, saturated or unsupported.

The system must never publish merely because a timer fired.

## 16. Engineering requirements derived from this research

1. Add a thesis object to the content pipeline.
2. Add an explicit information-advantage score.
3. Add a source-vs-analysis ratio check; headlines and source facts must not dominate the original analysis.
4. Add a semantic originality comparison against recent Square content and our own history.
5. Add a generic-question detector.
6. Add a mechanism/invalidation requirement for analytical posts.
7. Add a visual-purpose field and verify the rendered asset matches that purpose.
8. Add a human-voice / anti-template score.
9. Add a `WAIT` path when no candidate clears the high-value threshold.
10. Feed post outcomes back into hook, topic, format, visual and thesis selection.

## 17. Non-goals

The creator must not:
- fake engagement
- create or control fake accounts
- spam comments
- manufacture trading activity
- promise returns
- manipulate markets
- invent data or personal experiences
- weaken safety/integrity gates to force publication

## 18. North star

The target is not to produce the most posts.

The target is to make readers think:

> `I follow this account because it consistently explains things I would have missed.`

That is the creator identity that can support durable audience growth and eligible monetization.
